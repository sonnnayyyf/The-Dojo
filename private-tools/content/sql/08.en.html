Lesson 8 — Dates & time

Almost every data table has a date column: order date, sign-up date, birthday… And many business questions revolve around time: "orders in May?", "customers who joined in 2024?", "how many orders per month?".

In this database, dates are stored as 'YYYY-MM-DD' strings (e.g. '2025-05-01'). This format has a very handy property: comparing the strings is the same as comparing the dates — '2025-05-10' > '2025-05-03' works exactly as you'd expect.

This lesson uses two date columns: orders.order_date and customers.joined_at. You'll compare dates, filter by range, and extract year/month/day with strftime.

1. Comparing dates

Because dates are 'YYYY-MM-DD', you use the operators >, <, >= just like with numbers — remember to quote the date.

SELECT id, order_date
FROM orders
WHERE order_date >= '2025-05-05'
ORDER BY order_date;
Exercise 1. Get id, order_date of orders on or after 2025-05-05, sorted by order_date.

2. Date ranges with BETWEEN

BETWEEN works with dates just like with numbers — an inclusive range.

SELECT id, order_date
FROM orders
WHERE order_date BETWEEN '2025-05-01' AND '2025-05-31'
ORDER BY order_date;
Exercise 3. Get id, order_date of orders in May 2025 (BETWEEN '2025-05-01' AND '2025-05-31'), sorted by order_date.

3. Extracting year, month, day with strftime

strftime(pattern, date) pulls out a part of a date: '%Y' = year, '%m' = month, '%d' = day. The result is text (e.g. '2024', '05').

SELECT name, strftime('%Y', joined_at) AS year
FROM customers;
Exercise 4. From customers, get the join year: strftime('%Y', joined_at) AS year. Show name and year.
Exercise 5. From orders, get the order month: strftime('%m', order_date) AS month. Show id and month.

4. Filtering by a part of the date

Put strftime in WHERE to filter by year or month.

SELECT name
FROM customers
WHERE strftime('%Y', joined_at) = '2024'
ORDER BY joined_at;
Exercise 7. Get the name of customers who joined in 2024 (strftime('%Y', joined_at) = '2024'), sorted by joined_at.

5. MIN and MAX with dates

MIN/MAX work on dates — find the earliest and latest order.

Exercise 9. Find the earliest and latest order date: MIN(order_date) AS first_order, MAX(order_date) AS last_order from orders.

6. Grouping by time

Combine strftime with GROUP BY for "per month" or "per year" reports.

SELECT strftime('%m', order_date) AS month, COUNT(*) AS n
FROM orders
GROUP BY month
ORDER BY month;

Lesson 12 — Mini project: sales report

By now you have all the tools to do something very real: build a complete sales report. This is a consolidation lesson — each exercise is a piece of the report, together forming a full picture of the shop: revenue, best-sellers, top-spending customers, monthly trends.

You'll reuse everything: JOIN to connect tables, SUM/COUNT to aggregate, GROUP BY to split into groups, LEFT JOIN + COALESCE to leave no one out, ORDER BY/LIMIT to rank, and strftime for time-based reports.

Recall the revenue formula: each order line has a line total = p.price * oi.qty. Add them up to get revenue. Let's go.

1. The headline numbers

Every report opens with a few big numbers. Total revenue = sum of every order line's total.

SELECT SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS total_revenue
FROM order_items oi
JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.id;
Exercise 1. Compute total revenue: join order_items to products, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS total_revenue.
Exercise 2. Count the total number of orders: COUNT(*) AS order_count from orders.
Exercise 3. Count how many customers have bought: COUNT(DISTINCT customer_id) AS buyers from orders.

2. Revenue by category and product

Join then group to see where the money comes from.

SELECT p.category, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS revenue
FROM order_items oi
JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.id
GROUP BY p.category
ORDER BY revenue DESC;
Exercise 4. Revenue per category: p.category, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS revenue, grouped by p.category, sorted by revenue descending.
Exercise 5. Top 3 products by revenue: p.name AS product, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS revenue, grouped by p.id, sorted by revenue descending, take 3.
Exercise 6. Units sold per product, including 0 (use LEFT JOIN): p.name AS product, COALESCE(SUM(oi.qty), 0) AS units, grouped by p.id, sorted by units descending then p.id.

3. Customers

Join four tables to see how much each customer spent.

SELECT c.name AS customer, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS spent
FROM orders o
JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.id
JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.id
JOIN customers c ON o.customer_id = c.id
GROUP BY c.id
ORDER BY spent DESC;
Exercise 7. Spend per customer (buyers only): c.name AS customer, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS spent, grouped by c.id, sorted by spent descending.
Exercise 8. Customers who never bought: name from customers where id NOT IN (SELECT customer_id FROM orders), sorted by id.

4. Advanced metrics

Average order value = total revenue divided by the number of orders. Use a subquery for the order count.

SELECT ROUND(SUM(p.price * oi.qty) * 1.0 / (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM orders)) AS avg_order_value
FROM order_items oi
JOIN products p ON oi.product_id = p.id;
Exercise 9. Compute the average order value: ROUND(SUM(p.price * oi.qty) * 1.0 / (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM orders)) AS avg_order_value (join order_items to products).
Exercise 10. Revenue by month: strftime('%m', o.order_date) AS month, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS revenue (join orders → order_items → products), grouped by month, sorted by month.
Exercise 11. Revenue per category with a label: p.category, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS revenue, and CASE WHEN SUM(p.price * oi.qty) >= 100000 THEN 'high' ELSE 'low' END AS tier, grouped by p.category, sorted by revenue descending.

Exercise 12. 🔥 Stretch. Most valuable customer: c.name AS customer, COUNT(DISTINCT o.id) AS orders, SUM(p.price * oi.qty) AS spent (join orders → order_items → products → customers), grouped by c.id, sorted by spent descending, take 1.
